For our practice this week and during the Integration Month, we would like to invite you to experiment with a 500-year-old practice called Examen. It’s a method of daily self-examination in the presence of divine love that comes from [St. Ignatius of Loyola](https://www.jesuits.org/stories/the-life-of-st-ignatius-of-loyola/) of the Jesuit tradition. 

Ignatius's Examen was typically done as a daily practice at the end of each day. However, you can adapt this to be done once at the end of the week, or, in the case of this Living School journey, we invite you to do a larger, broader Examen in which you look back over not just a day or a week, but the whole first trimester. 

The goal of this practice is to become aware or conscious of your interior world and the ways that the divine has accompanied you. In any area of life, transformation requires growth in consciousness. The hope with this practice is that it will aid you in releasing your heart, hands, and mind from the day or week and enter rest. 

This practice is great to do with a journal in hand for 5 to 15 minutes at the end of the workday, to transition into your non-paid-work life; or at the end of the week in a longer form of 30 to 45 minutes, to transition into the weekend; or, in the case of the Living School, at the end of a trimester as you settle into the Integration Month. 

It is a time of settling, when the pieces of our hectic lives can be given the space to settle into place. 

The following five steps were modeled after the spirit of Ignatian Examen but are adapted a bit for our context. We invite you to play with them. Try practicing them this week and revisit them during the Integration Month as a means to intentionally give space for your learnings to seep down into the cracks of your life. 

1. _Become_ _Aware of Presence_ 

Settle in and still your mind and your heart. Allow yourself to be fully present to the moment you are in. Be present to yourself, present to Reality, present to Love, present to God. Remember that your work self, your task-oriented self, your relational self, etc. are not separate from the rest of who you are. Breathe deeply. Notice your body. Set your intention on clarity as you prepare to reflect on your day, week, or trimester. Ask God to fill you with the Spirit so that the Spirit can lead you through this potentially difficult soul searching. Otherwise, we may be liable to hide in denial, wallow in self-pity, or seethe in self-loathing. 

2. _Practice_ _Gratitude_ 

Begin by giving God thanks for all the things you are grateful for. Allow your mind to wander as you reflect on the ways God has blessed you throughout this day/week/trimester. Allow big things and small things to arise—everything from the gift of your faith to the gift of your marriage, to the easy commute to work today. Allow your heart to move to a place of gratitude—for your abilities; for the gifts, skills, and talents you bring to your spaces; for your connections; for who you are; for the world around you. Recall a few things that happened in the day/week/trimester that you are especially grateful for. Savor them. 

3. _Explore Consolations_ 

Review your day, week, or trimester from the beginning to the present moment, noticing what made you feel most alive. What energized you? What inspired you? What made you feel seen and heard and loved? What sense of accomplishment do you have? Pay attention to what is happening emotionally for you as you reflect on these things. Pay attention to your body. Notice if there are any themes or patterns. Write them down. 

4. _Explore Desolations_ 

As you reflect, there are likely moments rising to the surface that were challenging. What felt life-draining for you? Where did you feel more disconnected? Were there moments in which you felt particularly unproductive or unmotivated? Were there areas where you violated your conscience—actions or mistakes you might consider sinful or harmful? Were there ways that you fell short, gave up too soon, or pushed too hard? Pay attention to what is happening emotionally for you as you reflect on these things. Pay attention to your body. Notice if there are any themes or patterns. Write them down. Hold these challenges in the presence of deep Love and be gracious with yourself. Ask for help in sensing how to process these realities and move forward. Ask for wisdom to discern how you might handle such situations differently in the future. 

5. _Look Ahead_ 

As you turn to the final step, allow your heart, mind, and body to be released from your day, week, or trimester. Embrace the truth that you have done what is yours to do. Accept a sense of incompleteness. There are still things to be done, changed, or experienced, and that is okay. They can wait. Looking ahead to the evening or the weekend or the Integration Month, what are you most excited about? How will you rest? What does your body need? What do you want? Hold these things tenderly. 

## Examen summary
1. Presence
2. Gratitude
3. Consolation
4. Desolation
5. Look Ahead

## A note from Brian McLaren: 

In many communities that have been influenced by St. Ignatius of Loyola, the daily Examen focuses on two kinds of experiences, _consolation_ _and_ _desolation_. 

Spiritual director Vinita Hampton Wright offers an explanation of what these terms mean: Consolation means moving toward greater faith, hope, and love, and desolation means moving toward doubt, fear, and anger. [via](from [https://www.ignatianspirituality.com/consolation-and-desolation-2/](https://www.ignatianspirituality.com/consolation-and-desolation-2/)) 

A person dwells in a state of consolation when she or he is moving toward God’s active presence in the world. We know we are moving in this way when we sense the growth of love or faith or mercy or hope—or any qualities we know as gifts of the Holy Spirit. If I am becoming kinder with people, and I experience this movement as life-giving and Christlike, I am in a state of consolation. 

Consolation can hold many emotions and experiences. Consolation does not mean that I feel constantly happy or at peace. In fact, sometimes when I am doing precisely what God is leading me to do, I might feel negative pressure from others, or I might feel stretched or challenged because I’m growing and learning. Yet, if I sense in my spirit that I’m going the right way, this spiritual reality consoles me whether the day is bumpy or smooth. 

We dwell in a state of desolation when we are moving away from God’s active presence in the world. We know we are moving in this way when we sense the growth of resentment, ingratitude, selfishness, doubt, fear, and so on. If my outlook becomes increasingly gloomy and self-obsessed, I am in a state of desolation. I am resisting being centered in Divine love, joy, and peace or, if not actively resisting, I am being led away from my Center by other influences. 

Desolation also holds many emotions and experiences. If I’m in desolation, I might try to alleviate the discomfort by drinking too much or seeking distraction through more work or social events. The food and drink and activity might feel quite good, but they are not leading me to greater joy, peace, and love. In fact, “false” consolations can help me avoid the true consolation of God’s presence. 

Writer and spiritual director Margaret Silf offers these characteristics of consolation and desolation: 

### Desolation 

- Turns us in on ourselves 
- Drives us down the spiral ever deeper into our own negative feelings 
- Cuts us off from community 
- Makes us want to give up on the things that used to be important to us 
- Takes over our whole consciousness and crowds out our distant vision 
- Covers up all our landmarks. the signs of our journey with God so far
- Drains us of energy 

### Consolation 

- Directs our focus outside and beyond ourselves 
- Lifts our hearts so that we can see the joys and sorrows of other people 
- Bonds us more closely to our human community 
- Generates new inspiration and ideas 
- Restores balance and refreshes our inner vision 
- Shows us where God is active in our lives and where God is leading us 
- Releases new energy in us
