Now we get to live the resurrection. All year-round we get to do this. All the more now, in the season of Easter.

All Lent we prepared. You may have had your own personal practices. Together, we leaned into the stillness of God's abundant love and practiced listening together in the love-filled silence. The details of how (or how well) we did it are always less important than our sincerity. Always Christ is at work in us, usually hidden but always effective.

Love is the greatest sign of the resurrection. As we love, we live the resurrection.

And yet, as soon as we set out to love, we step on Legos of fear and hate. The table we mean to set with love ends in a sink of dishes dirty with fear and hate. Notifications of fear and hate ping at us from our phones. Often fear and hate make money and friends, but love is bad for business; it's not cool. Love lies bleeding, mauled by the jaws of fear and hate.

"You could say that Jesus came to reveal and resolve this central and essential problem," writes Richard Rohr in his book, *Dancing Standing Still*. He continues,

> I consider it the very meaning of the Risen Christ. There is really no other way to save us from ourselves, and from each other, until we are saved from our need to fear and hate. The pattern is so deep and habitual within humans that we even make religion into a cover for our need to be fearful and hateful.
>
> The ultimate disguise whereby you can remain a mean-spirited person is to do it for God or country. You are relieved of all inner anxiety; you can maintain your positive self-image and even some kind of moral high ground, while underneath are “the bones of the dead,” as Jesus said (Matt 23:27).

So why be either surprised or smug? We simply follow in the crucified and risen Christ's footsteps. To meet fear and hate (including our own) in the strength of love is to live the resurrection. Because where does the hope come from to love and not fear or hate? Doesn't it come from the promise of the resurrection? Or how would we know that love truly heals fear and hate apart from the resurrection? The tomb is empty and Christ is alive. The Divine Healer loves through us.

We know people who are living, breathing embodiments of the resurrection--who forgave in radical ways, who let go of their fear and anxiety and step out in faith, who choose joy and hope in the midst of life's difficulties. We too have a story to tell. We are students in a sacred lineage of love, learning day-by-day from our God-given teachers how to live the life-giving life of Christ.

Thanks be to God.

Pastor Clark Olson-Smith
