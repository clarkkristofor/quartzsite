1. There are two “Ways of Being”—the Responsive way where we see others as people, and the Resistant way where we see others as objects.
2. Way of Being is deeper and more important than behavior.
3. When we betray ourselves, we enter the Resistant way of being (the “box”).
4. In the Resistant way of being, we see others and ourselves in ways that justify our own self-betrayals.
5. Over a lifetime of self-betrayal, we develop characteristic self-justifying ways of seeing ourselves and the world; we carry our “boxes” with us.
6. From within the box, we can exhibit many different outward styles of self-betrayal, but all of them are fundamentally accusing.
7. Therefore, in the box, we provoke others to get in the box as well, and the mistreatment we then get from others gives us justification for staying in the box.
8. We get out of the box not by focusing on ourselves but by responding to others—that is, by receiving the humanity of those we have been resisting.
9. Responsive relationships and memories give us leverage to get out of the box in other areas of our lives. We get out of the box by finding an out-of-the-box place within us and then pondering the situation anew from that perspective.
10. Once out of the box, we stay out of the box by doing for others what we feel we need to do.

From [Diana Gabriel](https://www.dianagabriel.com/)
