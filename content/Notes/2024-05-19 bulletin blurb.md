# Blazing yet not Consumed
There is poetry in Pentecost, not least in the fire. This is poetic, symbolic, poetic, archtypical language. Asking skeptically, "But did it really happen?" or even to insist emphatically that it did indeed happen exactly like that is to fall into the trap of the modern, pragmatic, literal mind. Pentecost's wisdom is deeper than that shallow argument.

There is fire in each of us, a fire--if Acts is to be trusted--that is from God. This fire has many names: life-energy, passion, creativity, impulse, curiosity, hunger, desire, sexuality, anger, power, playfulness, *eros*, love.

Remember Moses? He was drawn to fire, which was the very presence of God. A burning bush, and "the bush was blazing, yet it was not consumed."

Here is powerful wisdom because we all know fire is dangerous. So is unbounded passion, insatiable hunger, and uncontrolled anger. Moses represents this. Witnessing an Egyptian slave master beating a Hebrew slave, Moses burned and was consumed, murdering the Egyptian.

But fire is also useful, necessary, life-giving. As is creativity, sexuality, and power, though we often fear and deny them. Moses also represents this. After committing murder, Moses fled into the wilderness beyond Egypt, became a herder, married. I sense subtext here. Moses doused the flame. Some depression was at work, which was more than clinical, the consequence of his dread fear of his own fire and his attempt to put it out. 

The danger goes both ways. These holy energies can consume us, yes, and dramatically. And much harm comes when we repress these energies and with them our very vitality. On this side, the end comes with a whimper, not a bang. 

Into this hot and then cold situation comes God, in a bush that blazes but is not consumed. A miracle to be sure, but all the more miraculous in this light: the burning bush is *who we can become*. 

In Acts, the fiery bush becomes fiery people. People who blaze and are yet not consumed. After the encounter with the burning bush, Moses dares (not without much divine prodding!) to liberate the chained and repressed fire within himself. This was necessary to become the kind of person who could liberate God's people from Pharoah. 

Such is the point of faith, spirituality, religion, inseparable as they truly are. To train and transform us in just this same way as Moses and Peter were. Not to quench the fire but to burn without consuming or being consumed. To become the kind of people who can bear holy fire over a lifetime and be embers of God's liberating, life-giving change in the world.
