[[my calling]] [[Zion]] [[inclusion statement]]

“And the chosenness doesn't have to do with [[belonging]] to the right group or meeting a moral worthiness contest because no one meets it…the cult of innocence. How can I prove that I'm not a sinner? I contrast that with communities of solidarity.”

Being chosen and beloved of God isn't about “belonging to the right group” or winning “a moral worthiness contest, because no one” does. No one can. We all fall short of the glory of God.

Faith isn't “a cult of innocence”—”how can I prove I'm not a sinner?” Instead, faith is following Jesus, dying to this need to be superior (or inferior) and rising to live with Jesus “with communities of solidarity.” 

Jesus in the gospels is always feeding and healing whoever needs it and loving and breaking bread with the wrong people—enemies, sinners, outsiders, foreigners. Nothing/no one good is all good, and nothing/no one bad is all bad.

So in Jesus's name and by God's grace, Zion seeks to be a community of solidarity with suffering and with sinners. Jesus said everyone will know we follow Jesus because of our love, not our anger or exclusion. “I will draw all people to myself,” Jesus said. Trusting Jesus does just this, [[Zion]] invites young and elders, rich and poor, women and men, cis and trans, gay and straight, darker skinned people and lighter skinned people, believers and doubters and seekers, human and animal and plant, land and waters and sky.

All are simultaneously sinner and saint, broken and beloved, both at the same time. “To each has been given a manifestation of the Spirit for the common good.” 

Let's seek and serve the Lord together.
