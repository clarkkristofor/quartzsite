In Trimester 1, we explored the Path of Contemplation. In Trimester 2, we explored the Path of Descent. In Trimester 3, we will now explore the Path of Love, notably, what it is to participate in loving action in the world. In the Living School we believe these three interweaving paths are the Essentials of Engaged Contemplation.    

The “and” that joins action and contemplation in our lives, the very same “and” for which Richard Rohr named the Center for Action and Contemplation in 1987, is the topic of discussion as we move into Trimester 3.   

Contemplation and action are the ongoing inhalation and exhalation of divine love coming alive in each of us. The free-flowing inspiration of loving contemplation and the free-flowing respiration of loving action together constitute our moment-by-moment rhythm of life. 

 A central guiding principle in our work in the Living School is that our personal healing is always in the service of the healing of the world. Beautifully, working for the healing of the world also contributes to healing our own hearts. For this reason, the CAC’s vision is to support “transformed people working together for a more just and connected world.” 

In our first short Deep Listening, Fr. Richard reflects on the theme of action and contemplation together as “the supreme Christian artform.” How we integrate them together is the important part. That's why we at the CAC often say that the small word "and,” hidden in the middle of our name, is more important than either action or contemplation, in and of themselves.   

In our next video teaching, Brian McLaren shares how the path of contemplation and the path of descent might lead to the path of love. He asks us: “Is it possible that if we continue to follow the contemplative path, it will take us to a place where we join God in God’s grief for the world—where we join the divine spirit in the groans and heartbreak for a world that could be so much better, or a world that often is being pulled back into things that it should have outgrown long ago?”  

We are reminded that this path is no mere hero’s journey, headed toward finding our own true self or fulfilling our own personal greatness as an individual. No—this is a healer’s journey, as guided by contemplative practices. On the path, we will walk through descent and do the work of our own healing, which will lead us to the medicine that can be employed for the healing of the world. As Carmen Acevedo Butcher clarifies, “We become the medicine for the healing of the world.” 

We become love.  

The paths of both contemplation and descent have led us here.  

In our next video this week, Fr. Richard and Jennifer Tompos explore just how that challenge and the labor give birth to the “both/and” thinking that is necessary not only to join action and contemplation, but to be able to love and live into adult spirituality. Fr. Richard tells us, “We come to wisdom by struggling with conflicts… [with] dissonance in the difference of things, the contradiction inherent in almost everything…. When you struggle with [paradox]—and I do mean struggle, not answer it…that is a recipe for ongoing growing up.”  

In our final Deep Listening conversation for this week, Carmen Acevedo Butcher and Mike Petrow have fun discussing “Wounding, Weird, and Wonderful Words”, exploring how our both / and thinking, and our entire journey through the Essentials Course invites us to expand our understanding of the words we use to discuss our journey and experience. We can “respect” our words precisely by re(in)specting them, and letting them expand as empower us in a learning that is all about discussing not defining, exploring not explaining, and being unafraid of the fluidity of letting our words be as wonderfully weird as we are.   

In our Deep Reading this week, we allow several selections of poetry to guide us into the land of the and. As author and Living School Alumna Felicia Murrell reminds us, the “and” is simply “the restorative power of love in an either/or world.”
