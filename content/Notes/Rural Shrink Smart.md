## Rural Shrink Smart
**promo: Introduction to the Rural Shrink Smart Initiative**
The Rural Shrink Smart Initiative is based at Iowa State University in the College of Design. The team has been developing new approaches to questions of rural quality of life in small and shrinking towns for about six years. Their work includes learning from and collaborating with a group of small Iowa towns that have stabilized and protected quality of life even as they continue to lose population. This workshop will focus on sharing the team's strategies to help communities shift their perspectives away from what has been lost to what we love about the places where we live, what we value about our communities, and how we can be better connected to each other and focused on the future even as we adapt to changing circumstances.

Professor Kimberly Zarecor and Professor of Architecture, ISU and National Science Foundation, and Professor Megen O'Toole Assistant Teaching Professor of Instructional Design, ISUIowa Shrinksmart, will provide overview of this initiative with an invitation with (date/location tbd) for congregations to dig into this material even more in a workshop later in the year. Shrink Smart
1. Purposeful collective action - process not product 
2. Communities need local champions - a few individuals can make a big difference, must happen from within 
3. A mindset of resiliency - building and maintaining relationship beyond family and friends - bonding, bridging, linking 

Beware economic development as a typical response - it's not a solvable problem - it's not your fault, you haven't done anything wrong - national and global economic trends

You can lose population and still find ways to thrive.

Shrinking smartly does not preclude future growth. Keeping current residents and members is first priority. Others who come will know there is joy here.
