# When Ascent Stops Working

Fr. Richard Rohr teaches us that at some point we will fail at something, or something will fail us.  

This initiates us into a completely different kind of wisdom. 

As we begin our journey together this week, we first invite you to read the following quotes from Fr. Richard's book _Falling Upward_ slowly and thoughtfully, noticing and taking note of what each stirs in you:  

> One of the best-kept secrets, and yet one hidden in plain sight, is that _the way up is the way down_. Or, if you prefer, _the way down is the way up_. This pattern is obvious in all of nature, from the very change of seasons. (xviii)  
> 
> Life, if we are honest about it, is made up of many failings and fallings, amidst all of our hopeful growing and achieving. Those failings and fallings must be there for a purpose, a purpose that neither culture nor church has fully understood. (xv)  
> 
> Sooner or later, if you are on any classic "spiritual schedule," some event, person, death, idea, or relationship will enter your life that you simply cannot deal with using your present skill set, your acquired knowledge, or your strong will-power. Spiritually speaking, you will be, you must be, led to the edge of your own private resources. (65)  
> 
> Normally a job, fortune, or reputation has to be lost, a death has to be suffered, a house has to be flooded, or a disease has to be endured. The pattern in fact is so clear that one has to work rather hard, or be intellectually lazy, to miss the continual lesson. (xix)  
> 
> There must be, and, if we are honest, there always will be at least _one situation in our lives that we cannot control, explain, change, or even understand_. (68)  
> 
> Until we are led to the limits of our present game plan, and find it to be insufficient, we will not search out or find the real source, the deep well, or the constantly flowing stream.... We do not really find something until we first lose it, ignore it, miss it, long for it, choose it, and personally find it again—but now on a new level. (67) 

In this second trimester, or season, of the Living School, we will explore “the path of descent,” the wisdom that comes from walking through the hard places, the desert, the Dark Night of the Soul—confronting suffering, failure, our own secrets and limits, as well as the limits, secrets, and blind spots of the institutions that shaped us, and then alchemizing all that into wisdom and medicine for growth and healing. 

We will be learning from Christian Contemplatives—ancient and recent—who confronted their own failures, the failures of the mainstream Christian culture, and the suffering of the world, and found a different path. Throughout Trimester 2, we will hear from a variety of new teachers, some offering wisdom from other contemplative traditions that is helpful for navigating the downward season of descent.  

Origen equated this particular “middle” season with the biblical book of Ecclesiastes—perhaps a season in which we take all our learnings and interrogate them with the hard and joyful realities of our own lived experience. 

If this is true, then Ecclesiastes seems to offer us some paradoxical counsel for navigating those moments when it feels like the ideological ground has been pulled out from under our feet. (We’ll address Ecclesiastes more later in the season.)  

First, we shouldn’t be afraid to ask hard questions or to lean into intellectual and emotional honesty about the curriculum of our lives.  In the midst of that, we should also be sure to re-ground ourselves in our breath and practice, in nature, in wisdom from sometimes-surprising sources, and, above all, in the spontaneous moments of joy that still blossom for us, even amid difficulty.   

As Origen wrote, _“Hopou Logos agie"_—Seek Divine Wisdom _wherever_ it can be found.  

As we set out on this search together, we will begin by **_Deep Listening_** to our Core Faculty as they share some of their own personal stories of navigating the path of descent.  

In our **_Deep Reading_** this week, we’ll explore Fr. Richard’s description of the path of descent from his book _Falling Upward_. In these selections, he explores the wisdom of “The Tragic Sense of Life,” "Stumbling over the Stumbling Stone,” and “Necessary Suffering.” We will also offer an optional additional reading from Brian McLaren called “Doubt as Descent.”

For **_Deep Practice_** this week, we will be continuing our daily sit, accompanied by a Thomas Merton prayer.  

There are a few **_Deep Reflection_** questions for you to ponder and journal on throughout your week. 

In addition, make sure to head over to your Pod and Circle to engage in **_Deep Interaction_**.  

Welcome to the path of descent. 

As you work your way through Trimester Two, we want to remind you that the Path of Descent is deep soul work that affects our entire person and our whole life. Please take extra good care of yourself this trimester, getting plenty of sleep, drinking lots of water, eating good nutritious food, moving your body, staying with your practice, getting out in nature, staying connected with your therapist and spiritual director—or finding one—and the life-giving relationships in the curriculum of your life. 

Try to be extra aware of how the material affects you in the fullness of your relationships and embodied world.  Please do not drive or multitask in unsafe ways when listening to potentially triggering material—it really does affect our reaction time. And lean into whatever gives you life. 

Each week we will share a team member’s personal experience with soul care. 

This week our producer Jenna Keiper tells us her three most helpful self-care practices are: 

1. Creating and sustaining a bedtime routine and being in bed by the same time every night.  
    

2. Scheduling "unstructured play time" into my weekly calendar—i.e. a date with myself where I follow my intuition and delight: I don't plan ahead or do anything "productive.” I allow myself to start and stop activities according to whimsy and inner attention. I am faithful to myself and consider this scheduled unstructured time sacred.  
    

3. Drinking 3 liters of water every day. (I track with a one-liter bottle I find beautiful - filling it up three times is a delight.)

#### Here is a prayer for your daily practice from Thomas Merton:

MY LORD GOD, I have no idea where I am going. 

I do not see the road ahead of me. 

Nor do I really know myself, and the fact that I think that I am following your will does not mean that I am actually doing so. 

But I believe that the desire to please you does in fact please you. 

And I hope that I have that desire in all that I am doing. 

I hope that I will never do anything apart from that desire. 

And I know that if I do this you will lead me by the right road though I may know nothing about it. 

Therefore will I trust in you always though I may seem to be lost and in the shadow of death. 

I will not fear, for you are ever with me, and you will never leave me to face my perils alone. 

Amen. 

 —Thomas Merton
