# How do you understand the connection between action and contemplation—the “eighth theme of the alternative orthodoxy”—so far?
Spring and stream is Elaine's way of putting it. Vine and branches is a biblical way. These resonate.

> I don’t know how you can do it without meditation. How could you go to that deep place where you do not want to publicly expose, humiliate, or defeat your opponents, but rather work, as King said, for win-win situations? Seeking win-win solutions, not win-lose, takes a high level of spiritual development and demands spiritual conversion. When you are hurt, you want to hurt back; when you are put down, you want to put down the opponent. I’ve met so many wonderful social activists. I often agree with their conclusions and with their politics, but, to be honest, I am often disappointed in their actual energy, goals, and seeming motivation. They seem to be as alienated, angry, and negative as are the people on the other side of the argument. 

[[Richard Rohr]], [[Dancing Standing Still Quotes]]

I feel like this here is a new learning or growing edge. Not like it hasn't been said, but I haven't internalized it. I've been much more driven than drawn. Superiority and fear. There's an honesty about this. An energy that has nothing to prove.

Space. Breathing space. This is what makes God apparent. And when God is apparent, then God can be heard and followed. Instead, when we don't pause, we become like Abraham and Sarah, who let fear get the better of them and took the wheel of the bus, using Hagar and persecuting Ishmael. It gets complicated story-wise. Not to wish Ishmael had never been born, but to say that contemplation lets God drive the bus. And action is being the bus, with energy, goals, and motivation that are God's and not only our own.

I've known pastors like Rohr says, alienated, angry, and negative.
