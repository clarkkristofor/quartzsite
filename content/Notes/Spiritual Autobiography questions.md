## Trimester 1
1. What has brought me to the Living School at this time? If the curriculum of the Living School is my life, what are a few key insights that my life has already taught me? How has it brought me here now?
2. How did I first learn about contemplation? What was happening in my life at the time? How did it interest or affect me?
3. How has making space for quiet played a role in my life? Who has taught me what it means to be a good and intentional listener, and what did I learn from them?
4. As I reflect on my life as a “spiritual autobiography,” what have been some of the major themes or important moments or events so far?
5. If I was to tell my life story, who would I want to tell it to? Who has been a good audience for me? Who has been a challenging audience?
6. What role has the Bible played in my life, for help and/or harm?
7. What has the term “prophet” meant to me—or how has it been used—up to this point in my life? How do I feel about that?
8. How do I relate to the prophet Jonah? Have I had a “Jonah” moment in my own life? Have I ever resisted a calling? Do I have a sense of calling now?
9. What is my experience with Jesus? Who or what has most influenced the lens I have seen and now see Jesus through? Who has he been in my life to this point? Who is he to me now?
10. What religious traditions have been major influences in my life? How have they been harmful, helpful, and/or healing? What stories or sacred texts have been comparable to scripture in my life? How have they influenced me?
11. What does alternative orthodoxy mean to me? What is my alternative orthodoxy?
12. Our teachers shared some of their own journeys through injustice and the wounds that have shaped their work in the world. Your journey is also uniquely your own, as are your wounds, your wisdom, and your work to do in the world: What has been your journey with injustice? Which world wounds particularly influence you, and do you feel close to healing or even called to help heal others? What wisdom has this offered you? What work does it call you to do?
13. What wounds, injustices, or sufferings in the world and in my journey do I feel most deeply? What wisdom is this leading me toward? What work is this leading me toward?

## Integration Month 1
1. Where are you from? This question can take you in many directions, reflecting on your birth, childhood, and adolescence: What geographical place are you from? What family system are you from—what set of ancestors or ancestries? What people were your mentors? What friends—and perhaps enemies or opponents—shaped you? What interests or hobbies shaped you? What economic, racial, and political realities shaped you? What personality traits emerged in your childhood?
2. What spiritual/religious traditions (or lack thereof) have shaped your life up until this moment? What role did the Bible (and/or other sacred texts) play in your life? In what ways was your religious or spiritual upbringing helpful and lifegiving? In what ways was it not helpful and lifegiving? What were some of your earliest spiritual experiences, questions, and struggles, and what did you gain from them?
3. In the one to five years before becoming a Living School student, what set the stage for you wanting to join the Living School community? What were the experiences of suffering or joy, struggle or insight that drew you? What books, teachers, and/or mentors made you spiritually hungry, thirsty, or curious?
4. Looking over your Deep Reflection responses for the previous trimester and reviewing the trimester content, what has happened to you over the last three months? How do you feel you are changing? Where do you feel you are stuck, breaking through, or being set free? You might find it helpful to use prompts like this: At this moment in my Living School experience, I feel ... I think ... I need ... I notice ... I am thankful for ... I am curious about ... I am troubled by ... I am looking forward to ....

## Trimester 2
1. During the integration month, what recurring season(s) did you notice in your own journey, and how have they impacted you?
2. What story of a season of descent has shaped your life?
3. Over your entire life journey, what have been your passing signs—or the confrontations with suffering or difficulty—that most woke you up? What are the passing signs or wake-up calls in your life right now?
4. What “desert” seasons in my life have invited me to rethink what matters most?
5. As you look back on your own life and the last few months of the Essentials Course, what rhythm of life or order of life has begun to emerge for you?
6. How have you experienced the dark night in your own life? What are you learning from that experience?
7. Where have you encountered the shadow of a spiritual leader or spiritual community? What action did it call you to? What are you learning from this experience? How are you healing from this experience?
8. What messages were you taught about grief growing up and how have they impacted you? How is grief real in your life right now? How does it feel in your heart and your body? What are you grieving, or what do you need to grieve?
9. Where have you experienced crisis contemplation in your life? Where have you witnessed others practicing and experiencing crisis contemplation?
10. What season(s) in life has driven you to ask hard questions? What were those questions? What hard questions are you asking right now?
11. What harmful narratives have informed the way you engage with the earth? How could you practice openness to reevaluating your relationship to the earth? What sort of action might that require from you?
12. As you look back on your life, even reaching into childhood, how has creativity been encouraged by self and/or others? How has it been discouraged by self and/or others? In the fullness of your own agency, how do you desire to play more like God?
13. When you look back on your life, can you identify any seasons of descent in your life? How has this trimester affected the way you understand those seasons now?

## Integration Month 2
1. When you look back on your story, do you see periods or seasons where "ascent" was working, you were learning and growing, or you felt successful and on track? How would you describe these seasons of your life? What did you gain? What did you lose? What emotions do you associate with these seasons? Who or what helped you during these times? Do you remember the times when that season of ascent, growth, and progress began to end? When, either suddenly or gradually, you felt that you were no longer gaining ground or moving forward?  What did you feel you were losing during one or more of those seasons? Looking back, what do you feel you might have gained? Who or what helped you during these times?
2. If you feel you have had at least one major dark night experience, describe it: how it began, what emotions you felt, what "bottom" felt like, what you doubted or questioned, who (if anyone) knew what you were going through and offered you support (or, perhaps, added to your pain). If you feel that you have not had at least one major dark night experience yet in your life (many people have not), describe how you will recognize it, how you would like to face it when it comes, and whom you might reach out to for support -- and why those people and how you would hope they would help you.
3. It is often in our descent that we are allowed -- or forced -- to face our shadows, the flip side of our strengths, or the hidden depths of our weaknesses. How would you describe your encounter with one or more of your shadows? We often are ashamed of our shadows, rather than realizing that they are part of us that must be faced, even understood and embraced. How are you now coming to understand the role that your shadows play in your life? How can they be seen as an unwanted gift, but a gift, nonetheless? Do you see any ways your shadows have helped you encounter divine love more deeply? If you don't feel that you have experienced an encounter with your shadows yet, you could talk about your experiences of grief in the same way. How would you describe your encounters with grief? We often are ashamed of our grief, rather than realizing that grief is a part of life that must be faced, even understood and embraced. How are you now coming to understand the role that grief plays in your life? How can grief be seen as an unwanted gift, but a gift, nonetheless? Do you see any ways your grief has helped you encounter divine love more deeply?
4. Looking over your Deep Reflection responses for the previous trimester and reviewing the trimester content, what has happened to you over the last three months? How do you feel you are changing? Where do you feel you are stuck, breaking through, or being set free? You might find it helpful to use prompts like this: At this moment in my Living School experience, I feel ... I think ... I need ... I notice ... I am thankful for ... I am curious about ... I am troubled by ... I am looking forward to ....

## Trimester 2
1. Which has been easier for you in life up to this point, the path of action or contemplation? How are you growing into bringing them together?
2. What is a catch-22, inconsistency, or contradiction you are facing in your life right now? How can you let it teach you wisdom by facing it as a paradox? What paradoxes have defined your life or taught you the most?
3. How has your view of the world changed over the years? How is your view of the world changing during your time in the Essentials of Engaged Contemplation Course?
4. What have been significant “unitive” or mystical experiences in your own life? When have you “fallen in love” with a person, place, pet, possibility, or powerful idea or understanding? What has it taught you about life and love?
5. How have you experienced yourself already living in “a crowded cosmos”? How might you live more deeply into that?
6. ???
7. What containers for identity, connection, and community have been life-giving in your experience and why? What containers for identity, connection, and community have been restrictive or harmful in your experience and why?
8. Where have you encountered “Creator’s instructions for humanity to live by” in science, in nature, and in the ancient wisdom traditions of the world?
9. What is easier for you—contemplation or action? How might you grow into whichever is harder for you?
10. How has the sound of the genuine shown up in your life? How have fear, deception, hate, and love shown up in your life? In what way(s) has engaged Christian contemplation offered you wisdom for fear, deception, hate, and love?
11. Who in your life is or was a good conversation partner or teacher from a culture, religion, or wisdom tradition other than your own?
12. What are some examples of wisdom from different world religions and wisdom traditions that have “leapt off the page” or “leapt into your heart” in meaningful ways throughout your journey?
13. At this point in the journey, what am I discerning is mine to do in service to the healing of the world?
