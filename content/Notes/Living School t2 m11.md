# Introduction

Buckle up! 

This week, on our journey through the path of descent, we are taking a virtual Living School fieldtrip. 

We are leaving the “classroom” and heading out on a winding backcountry drive to a small town called Yamhill, Oregon. 

In Yamhill and the surrounding areas, there are rolling hills of farmland, wineries, and very few people. You can almost hear the silence; it is so loud. All around us there are green hills, old farmhouses, and small bridges that pass back and forth over the river.   

As we drive closer, we take a left turn off the paved path and find ourselves on a dirt road. Just like at this point on the Living School journey, you may wonder if we are getting lost. We have been swimming around in the depths for about ten weeks. Perhaps you are asking the question of how long we intend to plumb the underworld of our spiritual lives.   

I promise: We are getting closer.   

The bumpy road beneath us offers a soundtrack to our journey. The dust storm kicked up by this gravelly road will clear, just like the cloudy, liminal spaces of some seasons of the spiritual life eventually begin to clear up, break open, and make way for the light to shine through. 

As we have explored throughout this trimester, sometimes our personal, private pain is what leads us into a path of descent. 

Sometimes, it is the suffering of our people—our family, our neighbors, our community, our religion or tribe or nation, or even our global civilization—that leads us toward descent. 

Sometimes, the pain of the living Earth itself draws us onto the path of descent. We are drawn into the sorrow of our fellow creatures, the ecosystems that surround us, the waters and air and soil that are the matrix in which the web of life trembles. 

When we “hit rock bottom,” we find that it is nature, creation—the world of animals and mountains and oceans and trees—that becomes our teacher. It offers us a solid foundation, something bigger than us, to root ourselves in.  

We invite you to join us this week as we descend into nature. 

How better to do so than to join our beloved teacher Randy Woodley on the plot of land that is Eloheh Farm and Seeds.  

There, nestled in the trees just to the left, is the driveway to a humble farm, hidden away but full of life.  

Pulling up to the house, you’ll see a porch with an open front door and a swinging screen. As we park the car and hop out, we are greeted by our gracious hosts, Randy and Edith Woodley. They are living out their calling to offer hospitality to the wanderers, the learners, and the earth-conscious. They will be our hosts and our teachers as we travel through the end of our trimester on the path of descent.  

Together, Randy and Edith run [Eloheh Indigenous Center for Earth Justice](https://www.eloheh.org/about-eloheh) and [Eloheh Farm and Seeds](https://elohehseeds.com/index.html) with the support of a few interns, the help of their sons, and the labor of a lot of volunteers.  

As Randy and Edith describe on their website, this is the work of Eloheh: 

> The climate crisis is increasing and urging us to respond. It is crucial we take action and respond in a way that is rooted in learning. Eloheh is founded by those who have long practiced a value for creation and listened to the voice of the Creator by stewarding what has been given to us. For decades, Randy and Edith Woodley have cultivated Eloheh (ay-luh-hay) as a community, farm, seed bank, and learning center. 
> 
> For over 30 years, the Woodleys have been teaching Native and non-Native people’s to deepen their commitment to preserving humanity’s role as good earth-keepers by understanding Creator’s invitation to harmony and shalom. Through summits, camps, ceremony, conferences and teachings, the Woodleys offer an opportunity for all to learn a new way to live on the land and respond to the climate crisis. 
> 
> Eloheh is a Cherokee word meaning harmony, wholeness, abundance and peace. Eloheh’s message is centered on restoring harmony (shalom) by increasing a spiritual consciousness of Creator’s concerns over earth justice. The Center will be a place to uncover diverse areas of life such as permaculture design, religious topics, plant medicines, seed saving, Indigenous spirituality, leadership mentoring, entrepreneurship, forums of racial equity and empowerment, community empowerment, ecological justice and Earth-care. 

In Randy Woodley’s formative book, _Shalom and the Community of Creation: An Indigenous Vision_, he outlines many of the differences between a Euro-American theology and practice and a Native American theology and practice.  

> The values of the Western worldview will not sustain us through the coming generations. The Western worldview is at odds with a sustainable earth. We must seek to convert ourselves to a more Indigenous worldview, with Indigenous values, if we hope to retain our place as co-sustainers on the earth. The earth will no doubt, survive. The question is, will we? 

Where Euro-American approaches are often redemption based, conquest oriented, bible centered, hierarchical, and lead toward reflection and affirmation of assumed viewpoints, a Native American approach is creation based, cooperative, ceremonial, mutual, and leads toward reflection and action. (Woodley, 110)  

Instead of continuing to simply read about these differences, let’s experience them by taking a step onto Randy and Edith’s farm.  

We will do this first through our **_Deep Listening_** conversation with Randy, Edith, and Jennifer that was recorded on their back porch overlooking the farm and the rolling hills beyond. Then, Brian McLaren gives us a teaching on sacred ecology in the vein of his new book, _Life After Doom: Wisdom and Courage for a World Falling Apart_. 

For **_Deep Reading_** this week, we will be exploring Randy’s book _Becoming Rooted: One Hundred Days of Reconnecting with Sacred Earth_. We will also read a chapter from Brian’s new book, _Life After Doom_. 

This week for **_Deep Practice_**, we will continue our daily practice. You will find a short video in which Randy Woodley reads one of his meditations from his book _Becoming Rooted_.  

**_Deep Reflection_** questions are available for you to sit with and journal on throughout the week. 

It is important to make sure you take your learning into your Pod and Circle and engage in **_Deep Interaction_**. Your guide is available for you should you need anything. 

We look forward to you joining us on our field trip. Enjoy!
