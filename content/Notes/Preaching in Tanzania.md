## 2nd Thessalonians 3:6-14
Now we command you, brothers and sisters, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, to keep away from every brother or sister living irresponsibly and not according to the tradition that they received from us. For you yourselves know how you ought to imitate us; we were not irresponsible when we were with you, and we did not eat anyone’s bread without paying for it, but with toil and labor we worked night and day so that we might not burden any of you. This was not because we do not have that right but in order to give you an **example to imitate**. For even when we were with you, we gave you this command: anyone unwilling to work should not eat. For we hear that some of you are living irresponsibly, mere busybodies, not doing any work. Now such persons we command and exhort in the Lord Jesus Christ to **do their work quietly and to earn their own living**. Brothers and sisters, **do not be weary in doing what is right**. Take note of those who do not obey what we say in this letter; have nothing to do with them, so that they may be ashamed.

- we are not paying for the food we eat here, it is your gift of love 
- in the companionship, we encourage each other to be active in love, receiving and giving

[Frank Crouch 2016](https://www.workingpreacher.org/commentaries/revised-common-lectionary/ordinary-33-3/commentary-on-2-thessalonians-36-13-2)
- Paul is not trying to establish social services policies for the city of Thessalonica. He is trying to establish a “do good for others even when you don’t have to” ethic among those who seek to follow Christ. He came to town, did not “have” to work, had the “right” as an apostle not to work, but, because he could work and contribute to the good of the whole, he did. ... If there’s something good that we can do, do it. If there’s something good we can give, give it. Even if we don’t have to. Even if we have a right not to.
- Rather than advising us to pursue ways to stop ourselves from helping others in need, or limiting our help only to those who prove they deserve it, Paul ends by tilting suddenly over to the other end of the giving continuum.

Indifference to suffering of neighbor. Sick and can't work. Unemployed. Shouldn't eat. Blaming the poor for their poverty. Lacking curiosity about neighbor. Keep their distance. When we know each other we know who wants to work and build up themselves and their community, and who doesn't. Who is paralyzed by hopelessness or a sense or worthlessness, and who is just irresponsible. 

In God's eyes, being truly willing to work is as good as working. Wanting to love is as valuable as loving.

Our companionship in Christ is founded in love. We are curious about each other. We have come near enough to each other to begin to know each other. I do not know all of you, but those of you I do know, I know are busy in love. You are working hard in love. You inspire me and encourage me to love more, to work for the Lord with more passion.

Zion people overjoyed to receive `your` letters. Encourage you to continue writing. Even in Swahili.

So it is easy to share with you. Food or anything else you need. Because your hearts and hands are busy for the Lord and for your brothers and sisters.

Now, we should always remember: The work of love is more than making an income. Sometimes the most loving people make no money because the world does not value the work of love. In the United States, people who work with children and elders are paid poorly. People like Neema who are employed teaching in daycare. Often they cannot support themselves or their families with their wages. It is not their fault. The wages are too low, because our society can be cruel and greedy. 

There is greed and cruelty everywhere, so I'm sure this happens in Tanzania too. 

Do not lose heart. Don't grow weary in doing what is right. Look at Jesus. He did nothing but show off the love of God, from his birth to his death to his resurrection. But were the rulers and the wealthy of Jesus's day eager to pay him a fair wage for this great work of love? Not at all. They repaid him with the cross. And so if we will follow Jesus, we must carry our cross too. Our treasure is in heaven. It's also right here on Earth, in a clean heart and in the love.

Many of you have worked in the new Zion Daycare construction. Some of you volunteer and make no wage. And what is your payment? It is love. It is the children who will learn and grow. Your children. Your neighbor's children. It is your community becoming stronger. Your church better able to do its work in Jesus's name. 

In Egypt, God's people slaved away for the Pharaoh. They suffered under his cruel whip. God heard their cry and sent Moses in God's own power. God set them free. Free from slavery and fear. Free for love. 

In building the daycare, you are living in God's freedom. You work freely in love and for love. You do not tslave away for a tyrant. In the Milk Store. And the Vocational Training Center. The donkeys of Marindi. The water project in Mpare. This work is its own payment, and the world knows nothing of it. God knows we need money, but there are billionaires who live in poverty without love. To work only for money. To work for anything but love is a great sadness.

But what a joy for us to work together, Kirangare Parish and Zion. Jesus came not to be served, but to serve. And Jesus serves us by bringing us together across an ocean to work together. 

You know we moved bricks for the daycare. And lay bricks. We laid tiles in Marindi. But among us travelers we joked, we mostly got in the way. Your expert builders did not need our help. 

The message here from Paul is love. We can never forget whenever God's Word is hard or confusing. The point is love. Start there and then work out the meaning. 

But some preachers and teachers do not know love. They may preach and teach from the Bible but they miss the point. Their message is not love. They only serve themselves.
