“I am the good shepherd,” Jesus said. This is a message of both comfort and challenge.

For centuries before Jesus, “shepherd” was a metaphor for kings and other leaders. Jesus calls himself a true shepherd—a leader who lays down his life for the people. Other kings and leaders Jesus called mere “hired hands,” because they lacked love and did not protect the people.

This word of judgement echoes Ezekiel 34, scripture Jesus surely knew. Notice God’s judgment against the shepherds—they’re acting like wolves, devouring the sheep! Corruption, abuse of power, oppression.

> To the shepherds—thus says the Lord God: Woe, you shepherds of Israel who have been feeding yourselves! Should not shepherds feed the sheep? You eat the fat; you clothe yourselves with the wool; you slaughter the fatted calves, but you do not feed the sheep. You have not strengthened the weak; you have not healed the sick; you have not bound up the injured; you have not brought back the strays; you have not sought the lost, but with force and harshness you have ruled them.
>
> ...Thus says the Lord God: I am against the shepherds, and I will hold them accountable for my sheep... I will rescue my sheep from their mouths, so that they may not be food for them.
>
> For thus says the Lord God: I myself will search for my sheep and will sort them out. … I will feed them with good pasture... I myself will be the shepherd of my sheep, and I will make them lie down, says the Lord God. I will seek the lost, and I will bring back the strays, and I will bind up the injured, and I will strengthen the weak, but the fat and the strong I will destroy. I will feed them with justice.

God promised to act and to create justice in society. In poetic language, God likens justice in society to food. “Give us today our daily bread,” Jesus taught disciples to pray. Like “I am the good shepherd,” this prayer echoes with fierce, justice-making love.

“I am the bread of life,” Jesus said. With “I am the good shepherd,” these echo with fierce, justice-making love.

Consider: sometimes forgiveness is not good news. Just as some leaders are not really leaders, some forgiveness is not really forgiveness.

God’s first act was not to forgive the wolves in shepherd’s clothing. They had sheep in their jaws! Forgiveness without justice is not really forgiveness. Instead, it blesses the abuse and enables abusers and oppressors to keep on abusing and oppressing. Sincere repentance includes true change—stopping the harm, restoring what was taken, and rebalancing power. Even God does not forgive without justice.

Jesus the Good Shepherd interrupts corruption, abuse, and oppression. The Good Shepherd does so without violence and killing, which the world calls justice but which is really just a mask for fear and hate, even when it claims to be religion. The Good Shepherd lays down his life not because the Good Shepherd is forced or lacks self-respect but for a justice-making purpose—to exorcise the demon of fear and hate within humanity.

Here is true justice, which includes forgiveness and yet is more. Justice is healing, not punishment. Medicine, not revenge. Life, not death. Jesus is more than a justice-maker, Jesus is justice itself. Because the one who said "I am the good shepherd" also said, “I am the bread of life." —PC
