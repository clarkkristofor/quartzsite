I felt an inner resistance when reading ch 18 of Life After Doom, and a fear of that resistance. Yesterday, over the weekend, I was wondering if the invitation was to share with a friend the fear? anxiety? I feel.

Make a moral choice not an intellectual risk assessment.

When I want me or something to be different. That's what the practices are for, days [[Gigi Ross]]. Not to change it fix but simply to see what really is.

I'm not alone. Others don't know what to do or how to be. Even a pastor in Brian McLaren. He worries about doom: climate disaster, racism and white supremacy, the rich getting richer and more powerful, authoritarianism. But I'm not alone. It's okay to not know what to do or how to be. It's not a character flaw or moral deficiency. Nothing to be ashamed of it afraid of. Not something that will get me kicked out of... What? QCI? OFM? I fear I'm doing nothing valuable, as I didn't know what to do or how to be. What would it be like to let go of that fear? Or to befriend it? Get curious about it?

[[Life After Doom]]: who this is for, who it's not for. His clarity of belief and values. Could I be that clear and forthright? Gut twist. I'm thinking about Facebook. Closing the Zion FB page? Then also the Everything Belongs podcast today: it's living life where the deepest prophetic. More so than strident speech. And from Saturday: "a prophet is someone who speaks needed truths in the process of being escorted off the premises." Hmm. That last bit is ringing hollow. 

Just before reading these final words in [[Falling Upward]]: "In the second half of the spiritual life, you are not making choices as much as you are being guided, taught, and led-which leads to "choiceless choices." ... You move from the driver's seat to being a happy passenger, one who is still allowed to make helpful suggestions to the Driver." I wrote to Annetta: 
	I'm in a deeper place. Even just in this final integration month. I'm seeing myself more clearly, shadows included, how driven by my insecurities I can be. I'm more tuned into the gracious Mystery, making it a little easier to coach myself into a bit more trust and patience.
	So it's like I'm both humbled and held. Like, wow I have a long way to go, but also mercifully able to keep the self recrimination at bay to kinda have some simple open curiosity about how this is all going to play out. I think because I trust I'll get there, or that the river will carry me somewhere good, so I can relax a bit more into experiencing the ride. I'm imagining like tubing down a river. Float on.
	Which is funny as I think about it because my word for 2024 was meander!

Calling? Celebrate [wineskin moment]( https://cac.org/daily-meditations/people-change-the-church/). And unorganizing the church, something Richard mentions in *[[Falling Upward]]*.

From finishing *Falling Upward*. When I push the river it's ego. How will the river wash away my insecurity? My pastor/contemplative/organizer persona? Learn to live at peace, he said. Being, not doing.

[[Oliver Burkeman]] calls is "insecure overachiever." This is much more accurate than enthusiastic overachiever. The felt need to be enthusiastic is also an achievement driven by insecurity. He  talked about stopping even though the work is not done. Resting, celebrating even though it is not all mastered and controlled and finished.

Being in the mood vs Getting in the mood vs What does mood have to do with fidelity? I'm the end it's who decides? Opting out when I don't *feel* like it is about retaining control. Doing it when I feel like it lets me feel good for feeling it. Can be ego driven. Let's me feel mastery. Showing up when 'nothing is happening' means I feel foolish, like I'm wasting time, doubt, great and anger: Where are you God? It's immaturity.

Since Wendy told me "the congregation" wants more direction, where are we going?, I've been off center and defensive. Part of me says I'm doing something wrong and need to change. That same part brainstorms. Enthusiastic Overachiever? Another part of me says I should be glad. Aren't I agnostic about direction? I just want coworkers? Don't freak out.

Since reading *[[Introduction to Internal Family Systems]]*, I've had mediating conversations with my polarized/extreme parts. Drawing out the sad part of me. The exile. EO is one extreme part.

While reading *[[Falling Upward]]*, I'm reflecting on:
- there's no forcing the movement from first to second half of life. it will be ego driven, not soul-drawn. grace, mystery. must finish well-enough first half of life tasks. page 14-15 -> leaving Cornell was not the right move, but finding a new way to be there was. i need to "change my major"? that process involved angst and "mistakes," "dead ends" (going to Africa) but mostly noticing how my inner self responded to the events of life (failure, two prelims on one night), catching myself in the act of knowing intuitively already what the right move was (elected as regional leaders for the semester I was planning to be away). I still feel embarrassed when I think about what my advisor saw in me. I look so chaotic, clueless, childish to "myself," to the PR part of me. But another part of me is so proud, because it was my listening to "the signal between stations" and my following what I heard that lead me in that zany way. I was chasing the butterfly, when most don't even notice the butterfly, let alone risk pursuing it. "If you're going to follow the Holy Spirit, you have to be willing to look like you don't know what you're doing," said Marcella? the instructor for the Group Spiritual Direction class at Moravian. And so here are two polarized parts: the embarrassed PR consultant and the proud butterfly chaser. One says, "Get your shit together. You're an embarrassment." And the other says, "But look, he was dancing to music only he could hear. How brave and beautify. And even you would agree, he made the right decision in the end. He couldn't have made that right choice without all the wrong ones before it." This only makes the other madder, because he loves right choices. "Make the right choice and look good doing it! Idiot!" What would happen if I didn't have you? "You'd become a wandering fool." Hmm. There's more here.
- Dad/church coopting structure/discipline/law as control, violence, abuse
- dismissing loyal soldier, who is enthusiastic overachiever
- What's my "kissing the leper"? Not "kicking the goad"?
