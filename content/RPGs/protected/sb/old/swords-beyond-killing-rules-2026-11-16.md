# Swords Beyond — Rules About Killing
*Compiled Nov 16, 2026, from the Nov 14–16 conversations.*

The paragraph in each section is what's decided. Anything not yet decided is listed under it and labeled. How a killing happens in the moves is left out, since that is already written into the rules.

---

## 1. The principle

Killing always feeds the shadow. There are no exceptions, because the game's moral compass depends on it. Hunting leads to killing or destroying, and that is the line: only the shadow and its monsters are hunted. Hunting exploiters feeds the shadow. Exploiters can be redeemed or stopped instead, and a victory can be mixed, with some redeemed or stopped and some destroyed.

## 2. Monsters and people

A monster is something that was never a person. Whether someone the shadow has fully collected is still a person is either always answered yes, or it is a distraction and a rationalization. It follows that ghosts, who were people, are quieted and not killed, that the shadow's followers and debtors are people, and that a demon, which was never a person, is a monster.

Not yet decided:
- *Working.* No separate "fight monsters" move. Reach, Act and Conflict cover a hunt, and what separates monsters from people is what you're allowed to want from them.
- *Candidate.* Monsters need their own rules for what must be true before they can be destroyed.

## 3. What a killing does

A hunter who kills cannot find safety. Something comes for them, and what it is depends on the killer and the Realm they serve. It comes from the shadow, since every Realm has a shadow of its own in how the cultures rooted in it go wrong. Until it is dealt with, that hunter has no downtime. This direction tracks nothing.

It fits because the hunter becomes the hunted, because downtime is where bonds refresh, wounds clear and hope is spent, and because getting safety back costs a hunt.

Not yet decided:
- *Working.* Each Realm has one killing complication. The six aren't written yet.

## 4. Solidarity

The rest of the pact can still find safety. Any of them may choose instead to stand with the killer, and you expect they usually will. Standing with them costs the same delay to downtime that the killer pays. Standing apart is no more awkward than any other time the party is split.

## 5. Text already written that carries this

- **Tagline.** A fantasy rpg about hunting monsters and caring for people, even the ones who act like monsters.
- **Hells culture stem.** "A wrong births a demon. A demon wrongs the wrongdoer. One becomes two becomes four becomes a plague."
- **Dead culture stem.** One with "purpose unmet cannot pass beyond" and remains as a ghost. "A vengeful one demands justice."
- **Lodestone blurb.** "Once you gave the shadow part of yourself, and now it hunts you."
- **The Garrison.** Bova calls everyone "brigands and monsters." The faction clock counts "monster-kill trophies."
- **The Seed.** "Return Captain Farker to the soil."
- **The title.** Swords are for monsters.

## 6. Tracking options, not ruled out

You said to keep these open alongside the no-tracking direction.

- **Shadow track** on the pact card. A box per killing.
- **Shadow die** on the table. Ticks up per killing. It could widen the shadow result: at 2, double 2s also count, and so on. Double 6s stay with the Powers.
- **Per-sign growth.** Killings advance the current shadow-sign.
- **On the killer.** A hunter who kills marks overshadowed.
- **Campaign shape.** A set number of shadow-signs to destroy, each needing a minimum number of hunts, with the die tied to the current sign.
  - *Tug-of-war:* starts mid-range, hunts tick it down, killings tick it up. Strongest at the start.
  - *Race:* starts at 1, ticks up every hunt and every killing. Destroy the sign before 6.
  - Your concern: a die tied to a sign is a countdown in practice and can't start at 6.

## 7. Shelved

- The consequence comes from the Realm itself and not from the shadow.
- The victim's Realm decides what's left behind.
- The land's Realm decides what's left behind.
- Only ghosts and demons as consequences. It would make the game about two Realms.

## 8. Open questions

1. What comes for a hunter of each Realm? Six to write.
2. What ends it?
3. When the GM kills by another's hand, what follows? Is that person hunted? Does it ever cost the hunters?
4. Does killing by anyone feed the shadow, or only killing by hunters?
5. Does "feeds" need to mean anything at campaign scale, beyond the pursuit?
6. When does a person die? Wound rules are unwritten, and death is "in the hands of fate" for hunters.
7. What is a monster made of?
8. Does a hunter's own sacrifice at Face Your Doom count as a killing?
9. Does going for broke in a fight with a person risk a death on a 9−?
10. Are the six killing complications the same six as the shadow gifts and the culture stems' far ends?

## 9. Text to revisit

- **Magic.** Killing by magic feeds the shadow like a sword does.
- **Abilities that kill or decree death.** Wrath, Poison, Death's Ground, Visions of Death ("when you kill a monster of shadow" is fine; naming who will die may not be).
- **Zealot.** "The sacrifice will be perfect," if the offering is anyone but themselves.
- **Downtime.** A hunter with no safety takes no beats. The chapter should say so when it's rewritten.
- **Move examples.** None shows a fight that ends without a death.
- **Hunted, the faction status.** Being cut. The word is now free for this.
