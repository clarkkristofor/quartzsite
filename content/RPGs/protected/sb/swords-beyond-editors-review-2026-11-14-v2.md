# Swords Beyond — Editor's Review (v2)
*Nov 14, 2026. Rewritten after the morning's conversation. Based on the core rules (project copy dated Nov 14), the Nov 13–14 session report, and what you decided today. Replaces the first review.*

## 1. What the game is about

**Your intent, confirmed:** Hunters defend an exploited borderland from a shadow that feeds on exploitation and killing, helped by Powers whose graces call hunters higher.

**Sharpened by today:** Hunters are people a Power cares about. They live up to it by caring for each other and for the Over Reach. The shadow is always near, offering relief from what that costs.

Hunters defend an exploited borderland from an insidious shadow. This shadow feeds on and fuels more exploitation and killing. Powers of the Realms Beyond give the hunters their graces and call them to hunt shadow and care for people and not to confuse the two.

**Decided today**

- There are two kinds of owing. What you owe your Power and your fellow hunters is earned: because/therefore, a calling. What the shadow and the exploiters claim is imposed: if/then, a transaction.
- Favor is given in advance. A charge is a calling, not a bill.
- The shadow is a loan shark and the ultimate exploiter. It always comes as a savior. It cares about no one, including the exploiters it has "given" the most. "Gift" is sarcastic.
- The Powers care about you, the people of the Over Reach, and even the exploiters.
- Refusing the shadow has to cost. The shadow is there to relieve the cost.
- Killing always feeds the shadow. Hunting leads to killing or destroying, so only monsters are hunted.
- A monster is something that was never a person.
- Exploiters can be redeemed or stopped. Victories can be mixed.
- The shadow lurks. It strikes or tempts on double 1s, when you're overshadowed, and at fate moves.
- The Maul may be cut or heavily revised.
- Each Realm gives its own grace (section 6).

**On the page today:** a game about borrowed power and what it costs. That is the wrong story by your own account, because favor isn't lent. The mechanics can stay. The words around them tell the loan story about the Powers, and that's the main alignment problem now.

**One wording note on your intent sentence:** "Powers that ask something in return" is if/then. Your thesis is because/therefore. Something like "Powers that call them to more" says what you mean.

## 2. What changed from my first review

- **Debt is not the spine. The difference between two kinds of owing is.** Withdrawn: renaming trust as what the town owes the pact, and treating bonds as a ledger.
- **"Are the hunters the swords?" No.** A Power that wields you contradicts the thesis. The better reading of the title is now available: swords are for monsters.
- **"How is the shadow beaten?" has an answer in concept:** it's starved. It has no answer at the table yet.
- **"Constant temptation" has a home:** the sword. Killing is the easy way out, and it feeds the shadow.

## 3. Alignment check against the thesis
- [x] **Title**: Swords are for monsters. “Beyond” is the Powers.
- [x] **“a fantasy rpg”**: The place to state the premise.
- [x] **Intro**: “Salted pork, the Garrison, and civilization” is the unasked gift that becomes a claim. Add the exploiters to “defeat the shadow.”
- [x] **Realm write-ups**: _Partly._ None names its grace. The Hells text (“only chaos, corruption, pestilence, and woe”) leaves no room for justice.
- [x] **Goals**: Should be the thesis in three lines.
- [x] **Favor**: _Mechanics fit, words don’t._ A track that fills until the Power comes reads as a tab.
- [x] **Fate moves**: 7–9 is flattery against care. 6− is the imposed gift. “Reward” is a transaction word for the end of a calling.
- [x] **Double 1s, overshadowed**: “Act in the interests of the shadow” now has content: kill, take its relief, collect its debts.
- [x] **Help, Protect, Anchor**: You spend bond to give, not to get.
- [x] **Bonds text**: Rapscallion’s “lean on those relationships to get what you want.”
- [x] **Basic moves**: No move knows that killing matters.
- [x] **Kin and cultures**: Kin who “eat shadow” and “steal from the shadow” fit a lender. The culture stems already hold the counterfeits.
- [ ] **Role blurbs**: _Mostly._ The Law speaks ledger language on purpose and sits at the seam. The Maul contradicts.
- [ ] **Abilities**: _Mixed._ “Owe the Hells a favor” and Devil’s Bargain make Powers and hunters into creditors. Several point lethal force at people.
- [x] **Pact**: A pact is a covenant, not a contract. The “unspoken pact with the people” is an earned obligation. Keep it.
- [ ] **Downtime**: _Contradicts in places._ “Take a monstrous ability,” The Descent, Deal with the Devil.
- [ ] **GM moves**: Nothing about the shadow’s offer or the easy kill.
- [ ] **Factions**: _Carry it by accident._ Bova calls everyone “brigands and monsters.” The Garrison counts “monster-kill trophies.” The Seed plans to “return Farker to the soil.” All three are now shadow hooks with no rewriting.

**Verdict:** the thesis is already in the fate moves, the teamwork moves, the intro and the faction situations. It is missing from the basic moves and the GM chapter, and it is contradicted in the vocabulary around favor, in some abilities, and in the Maul.

## 4. Strengths

1. **The 7–9.** The one who cares tells you no. The one who doesn't tells you yes. Everything today grew from this.
2. **Killing always feeds the shadow.** A moral rule that isn't a stock fantasy one. It makes a sword game where the sword is the temptation.
3. **Power charges, shadow gives.** Now with a reason: calling against transaction.
4. **Teamwork moves.** Bond is spent to give, and it renews on its own. Shadow gifts only compound. The rules already show the two kinds of owing.
5. **The six graces.** They keep the Realms distinct and give the Powers different faces.
6. **Character creation.** The tables tie hunters to each other, to the Cities and to the shadow.
7. **Reach.** Still the one basic move that feels like this game.
8. **Faction situations.** In motion, interlocked, and now loaded with shadow hooks.
9. **Overshadowed.** Corruption as play.
10. **Role blurbs and the drive formula.**

## 5. Weaknesses

1. **"Feeds" means nothing at the table.** The whole moral rule rests on a word with no rule behind it. This is now the most important gap in the game.
2. ~~**The rules never say when a person dies.** If killing feeds the shadow, death can't be a side effect of wound counts. Can a hunter kill by accident, on a 9−? Can "something or someone else pays" be a death?~~
3. **There is no hunt.** Still true. Shadow-signs are still the answer and still not in the rules.
4. ~~**The Powers read as creditors.** Favor fills like a tab, the end is a "reward," and three abilities let you "owe" a Realm.~~
5. **The shadow makes one offer.** Double 1s is a hook and overshadowed is a compulsion. Only the fate move tempts. That's fine if the sword carries the daily temptation, but then weakness 1 has to be fixed first.
6. **The Cities' servants have names and the people don't.** It matters more now. Exploiters have to be people you could redeem, and the people have to be someone you could care about. The Tenders have no one.
7. **The after-roll menu likely causes the conditions problem.** Unchanged from the first review: unaided hits run 17% to 58%, most hits are bought, and a condition is the cheapest purchase.
8. **Undefined terms.** Hope, the fate stat's range and start, trust, armor, sanctuary, the six shadow gifts, how wounds work.
9. **Bump in the Dark leftovers.** The session's list plus: Help Dice, steady/encourage/ground, "+1d," scale, tier, quality, solace, strings, fortune rolls, "fate rolls 9−," playbook, connection, Reach's "(mark 1)."
10. **Smaller:** ~~the conditions cascade states two rules that disagree.~~ Hound and Hex are listed but unwritten. The Lodestone prompt in the rules says "whole… whole" where the session says "completely."

What follows from the table:

- **Vengeance is hunting exploiters.** A Hells hunter lives nearest the rule about killing. So does the Law.

- **Curiosity can stop and hunger can't.** That is the humility the Deeps text asks for.

## 7. Unrealized potential

- **The six shadow gifts are the six counterfeits.** Wield Your Gift calls for a list of six that doesn't exist yet. You may have just written it.
- **Each grace shapes its charges.** A Deeps charge is a question to pursue. A Hells charge is a wrong to set right. A Heavens charge is someone to forgive. The fate scenes would feel different Realm to Realm.
- **Belief is a statement about your Power's grace.** The belief prompt asks how your Power will help. The graces give players a vocabulary, and flattery gives the shadow its script.
- **Shadow-signs as the hunt.** A monster that was never a person, with followers who are people, and two factions in the shadow's debt. Destroy the first, don't kill the second, release the third.
- **The shadow lends.** Your three GM options (extraction opens the way, it feeds on injustice, it offers what the Cities withhold) can sit under one question: what did it lend, and to whom?
- **The hunters mirror what they fight.** The shadow comes as a savior. The Cities came with civilization. The hunters come to Lost Plenty from elsewhere to defend it. What makes them different is the thesis: they don't collect. The game could ask players to prove it.
- **Refusing is half of winning.** The other half is the Law's drive: true debts paid. False debts refused and true ones honored is a fuller picture of the Over Reach after the pact wins.
- **The Garrison's decades of killing.** If killing by anyone feeds the shadow, the conquest and every crushed rebellion are its history in the Over Reach.
- **Conviction against truth.** Still unconnected: Revelation and Reach's "learn something true" are where the players' guess meets the GM's answer.
- **The first conquerors' kin.** Still unasked: one hunter's people carved out the Over Reach.
- **Move examples.** Twelve of them, none set in the Over Reach, none showing a fight that ends without a death.
