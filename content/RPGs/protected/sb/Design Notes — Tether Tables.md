Working notes on rules, tone, and constraints discovered through drafting. Meant to sit alongside _The Realms Beyond_ and _Tether Tables_ as a reference for consistency, not as content itself.

## Categorization test: Link vs. Mark vs. Omen

- **Link** — something you _have_: a physical object or an attendant being.
- **Mark** — something you _are_: borne in or on your body or soul. This includes traits whose _trigger_ is external, as long as the effect happens _to/in you_ — e.g. "you hear nearby prayers" is a Mark, not an Omen, because the perception is internal even though the source is outside you.
- **Omen** — something that _happens around you_: the world reacting, visible to others. If it describes what other people or things do in your presence, it's an Omen.

**Mark with external-facing consequences.** A Mark can produce effects that reach or land on other people, as long as the tether's effect _originates_ as something happening internally to you. "Your dreams carry messages for the living" stays Mark: the dream itself is the internal event, and the fact that its contents later reach others doesn't change where the effect starts. Contrast with pure-Omen entries where nothing internal occurs at all — e.g. "no one can place where you're from" or "crones you've never met already know you" are entirely about others' perception, with no internal event to anchor them as Mark.

**Worked examples from borderline calls:**

|Entry|Looked like|Actually is|Why|
|---|---|---|---|
|"doves" (bare noun)|Omen|Link-shaped|No reaction described — reads as an attendant being, not an event|
|sweet/rotten smell|could be either|Mark (as written)|You emit it — an Omen version would need reframing as others' perception|
|dark moths fly from your mouth|Omen (visual/dramatic)|Mark|Originates from your body|
|an older you tells you impossible secrets|Mark (as placed)|Mark, but Link-shaped|Implies a recurring encounter/entity, closer in weight to an attendant being than a passive trait — kept as Mark by precedent|
|no one can place where you're from|looked like Mark (about your origin)|Omen|Entirely about _others'_ failure to categorize you, not something happening in/to you — same shape as "crones you've never met already know you"|
|flowers near you bloom fast, then wilt fast|proposed as Mark (a duality, like the old sweet/rotten smell)|Omen|Describes the world (flowers) reacting near you, not something borne in your body|
|your dreams carry messages for the living|could be Omen (effect lands on others)|Mark|The dream is the internal event; delivery to others is downstream of something happening in you, not a description of the world reacting to you|

## Per-realm tonal registers (beyond the core write-up)

These are interpretive decisions made in practice, not necessarily obvious from _The Realms Beyond_ alone:

- **Dead** — reverent, not fearful. Fear is reserved specifically for the envious/resentful dead who refuse rest; ancestors and the "rested" dead are protective, not sinister.
- **Weft** — "wondrous and dreadful," leaning toward logic quietly failing rather than showy magic. Steered explicitly _away_ from body-horror/gore and toward loss of control, paradox, and things behaving almost-but-not-quite normally (doors, mirrors, repeated words, doubled selves, an autonomous small-space or object). Directly inspired by Alex Pheby's _Cities of the Weft_ trilogy (_Mordew_, _Malarkoi_, _Waterblack_) — a world where reality is literally woven from a material called "weft." Pulled concepts filtered through this quieter register rather than the source's grosser/more violent material: a companion born strangely or assembled from what's at hand, a secondary "wrong" power with a cost, a pocket space that shouldn't hold what it holds, an attendant that moves with no visible operator.
- **Heavens** — pushed past "gentle blessing" toward scrutiny and judgment — the "corners of the Heavens adjacent to the Deeps" angle from the write-up. Entries can unsettle (confession, unwanted clarity) without crossing into Hells territory. Also steered away from anchoring too heavily on one real-world tradition (see Religious/Cultural Specificity below) — the source text's "our gods" (plural) supports a blended or invented-pantheon feel, matching how the other five Realms don't lock to a single real-world religion. Heavens' worship stem (see Culture_Stem_Design_Process.md) extends this register into the pantheon itself: the core six gods deliberately span from unambiguously heavenly (herald, judge) through the dark/heretical (maw) and ambiguous/mutable (trickster) — the Deeps-adjacent territory named in the write-up lives _inside_ the core pantheon rather than needing a separate heretical-gods list.
- **Deeps** — buried riches, incomprehension, leviathans, mysteries between the stars, dread of awakening horrors. Room here for restrained cosmic-horror imagery (a contained, watching intelligence; something whose scale doesn't match its container) as long as it stays plain-language rather than reaching for genre-signaling vocabulary.
- **Hells** — rage, wickedness, chaos, corruption, pestilence, and — importantly — the _necessity of careful binding_. Binding/restraint/containment imagery (rings, chains, contracts, wards) belongs here more than in other Realms; if a binding-themed object surfaces while drafting another Realm, consider whether it's actually a better fit for Hells.

## Standing constraints

- No mechanical timepieces (clocks, etc.) — wrong-genre note for the setting.
- Weft: no more shadow/reflection entries — already well-covered (shimmer, heterochromia [since replaced], mirrors-elsewhere Omen); further entries in that vein risk repetition.
- Heavens: the core pantheon stays fixed at **6 gods**, deliberately asymmetric in tone (see tonal registers above). "Primarily" in the worship stem's phrasing is the sanctioned escape hatch for additional or local gods a GM/player wants to introduce — it is not a gap that needs filling with a larger authored roster.

## Style patterns (learned through revision)

- **Passive event over transactional utility.** "A book that opens on its own" reads devotional/eerie; "a book that opens to the passage you need" reads like a search function answering a query. The second implies request-and-response logic, which is functional rather than atmospheric. Prefer entries that describe something happening rather than something serving a purpose — this applies across Realms, not just Heavens.
- **Naming the mechanism isn't automatically weak — cliché is the actual problem.** "Threads of reality" and "when you write, the words squirm" both state plainly what's happening and work fine. What sinks an entry is a _worn_ image ("tear in reality" — overused in portal fiction/game marketing) rather than directness itself. Test an entry by asking whether the phrase is doing specific work for this setting, or whether it's a stock phrase imported from elsewhere.
- **Avoid generic genre-label nouns.** "Elemental spirit," "chaos spirit," "spark of living magic" name a _category_ rather than showing a specific image, and could belong to any Realm or any generic fantasy setting. Replace category labels with a concrete, particular image (e.g. "a companion built from whatever's nearby — leaves, dust, spilled water" instead of "elemental spirit").
- **Institution-naming too-on-the-nose trap.** A named institution that's just a synonym of the role or god it's attached to (judge → tribunal, trickster → wager-shrine, weaver → loom-order) reads as zero added information — it restates what the name already told the reader. This is the same failure as the generic genre-label trap above, applied specifically to institution-naming rather than object-naming. Push toward institutions that complicate or invert the obvious reading instead (see inversion device, below) — an institution should tell the reader something the role's name didn't already give away.
- **Modern/anachronistic language isn't automatically disqualifying; franchise-echo is.** "Hymnic earworm" was kept deliberately despite being a modern colloquialism. "Blessed ocarina" was cut because it read as a specific game reference (Zelda), not because it was modern. The test is whether a phrase pulls the reader toward a specific outside property, not whether the vocabulary itself is old-fashioned.
- **Self-explanatory vagueness vs. under-specified vagueness.** "Recurring dream" is a complete prompt as written — the player fills in _what_ recurs, but the phrase itself names a real thing. "Insatiable craving" (since replaced) named _no_ thing — "insatiable" only describes intensity, not an object or category, so there was nothing for a player to answer. Test: can the entry be restated as a question the player could actually answer in character creation? If not, it likely needs an anchor.
- **Real-world tradition/name-echo entries read as clever, not on-the-nose, when they're not clichés.** "Threads of reality" and "a lightning spider" (Weft), "iron ring" (Hells binding theme) all tie back to their Realm's core concept and work well _because_ the underlying image isn't overused. This is a case where deliberately naming the theme succeeds, contrasted with the cliché caution above — the difference is freshness of the specific phrase, not whether it echoes the Realm.
- **Inversion as a deliberate device.** "Beggars give you alms" (Heavens Omen) and "uninvited penitent, overeager and petty" (Heavens bench) both work by flipping the expected direction of a devotional/charitable interaction — devotion becomes unwelcome, receiving becomes stranger than giving. This is doing real work for Heavens' "scrutiny, not gentle blessing" register specifically: it unsettles without crossing into Hells territory. Worth reusing deliberately elsewhere a Realm's "expected" interaction could be flipped, rather than reinventing the device each time it's needed. The Heavens worship stem's inquest/generosity pairing (see Culture_Stem_Design_Process.md) is a variant of this device operating _across_ independently-rolled columns rather than within a single entry — see "emergent meaning at the point of combination," below.
- **Emergent meaning at the point of combination.** When a stem uses independently-rolled columns that combine into one sentence (see the Heavens worship stem), an entry can be written tonally neutral or positive on its own, with a darker or ironic reading surfacing only from a specific combination — rather than writing the edge into the entry's text. Example: "share grain, coin, and labor generously" stays purely warm as written; paired with the inquest as leading sect, the same line reads as _policed_ generosity, without either entry needing to say so. This extends the indistinctness-as-reinforcement device (see below) into cross-column territory — the ambiguity/friction is generated at the table, not authored in advance.
- **Oscillation phrasing: "X slipping into Y and back."** "Wordless singing, harmony slipping into dissonance and back" (Wild Omen) — a reusable construction for entries where two states trade off rather than one permanently replacing the other. Distinct from the existing sweet/rotten-smell duality phrasing (two fixed states named side by side); this construction implies ongoing motion between states. Reach for it when a duality entry risks reading as one-directional (e.g. an earlier draft, "harmony slipping into dissonance," was rejected for implying a one-way decline) but the intent is genuine back-and-forth.

## Cross-table checks (do before finalizing a new entry)

- **Object-family duplication within a single Realm.** Two gem entries turned up in Deeps (Link and Mark) four rows apart — close enough to read as repetition even though the columns serve different functions. Check a table for repeated core nouns/materials before locking an entry in.
    - **Caveat: doubling isn't automatically a cut.** Two later cases — Weft's thread motif (spindle that spins on its own + "you keep tripping over the threads of reality") and Heavens' wing motif (disciplinary bundle of eyes, mouths, and wings + vestigial wings of mottled feathers) — were kept deliberately as reinforcement rather than treated as the Deeps-gem problem. The difference: does the second entry deepen the Realm's signature image (thread for Weft, wings for Heavens-as-judgment), or does it just repeat a noun with no added resonance? Flag doubling for a judgment call, not an automatic swap.
    - **Further precedent: Deeps' "indistinct thing in the dark" pairing.** "Blind, hairless burrow-thing" and "something in shadows" (both Deeps Link) were kept side by side deliberately — both lean into deliberate imprecision as a source of dread, reinforcing Deeps' "beyond comprehension" register rather than repeating a single noun. Same logic as the Weft/Heavens doubling above, applied to a shared _quality_ (indistinctness) rather than a shared object.
- **Object-family overcrowding across Realms.** Keys (Deeps), coins (Hells), thread (Weft) each already anchor a different Realm. Consider whether a new object choice for another Realm risks diluting an existing Realm's signature material.
- **Column balance for attendant beings vs. inert objects.** Link columns should have a reasonable mix of attendant beings (creatures, companions) and objects within each Realm. Heavens initially had only one attendant being (berobed child) against 2–4 in other Realms — worth a periodic count-check per Realm.
- **Light-source clustering.** Heavens had three light-related entries accumulating (thurible "always lit," candles Omen, vial of starlight) before a fourth (a lamp) was caught and avoided. Worth flagging when a Realm is quietly leaning on one sensory device repeatedly.
- **Thematic overcrowding across a Realm's own stems (extends object-family overcrowding).** Surfaced while drafting Heavens' worship stem: an early instinct to spin off standalone "war support" and "death-escort" stems for Heavens ran into the same overcrowding problem as a repeated object-noun, but at the level of an entire function rather than a single item — those functions were already fully owned by Hells and Dead respectively. Standalone stems claiming a function another Realm's whole table already covers read as redundant, not additive. See "stem enrichment via paired attribute" in Culture_Stem_Design_Process.md for how this got resolved.

## Religious/cultural specificity (Heavens-specific, may generalize)

Heavens initially skewed toward specifically Catholic/Orthodox liturgical vocabulary (thurible, icon of a saint, blessed loaf, berobed child, prayer book) more than any other Realm anchors to one real-world tradition. Revision pulled some entries toward a blended or invented-pantheon register — pieces adapted or considered from Hindu-adjacent imagery (many-armed soapstone figure), Buddhist-adjacent imagery (a bowl never empty, incense burning without being consumed), and Islamic-adjacent imagery (mercy/judgment, almsgiving) — while avoiding literal reproduction of specific sacred objects or rites from any one tradition. General principle: draw broad motifs (abundance, mercy, contained divinity, watchfulness) rather than named liturgical objects specific to one faith, unless a Realm is intentionally meant to evoke one tradition directly.

**Generic-enough-to-clear vs. specific-enough-to-collide.** A term isn't an automatic collision just because a real-world specific instance of it exists somewhere (e.g. Orthodox Judaism, historical Inquisitions). The test is whether the term _specifically_ pulls a reader toward that one referent, or whether it's broad and generic enough that its plain-language meaning reads first. Heavens' sect names "orthodox" and "inquest" were checked against this and both cleared it — "orthodox" reads as "doctrinally correct/established" before it reads as any one tradition, and "inquest" is a mild, generic legal term (coroner's inquest) rather than a loaded historical proper noun like "the Inquisition" or "crusaders" (the latter was cut from this same list for pulling too specifically toward one historical conflict). See the parallel real-world term/sensitivity collision check in Culture_Stem_Design_Process.md, which this note extends.

## Historical/real-world grounding (may generalize beyond Wild)

Pulling from real-world history for grounding doesn't automatically dodge cliché — the "historical" image itself can already be the over-familiar one. Case in point: "druidic chanting" (Wild Omen, since cut) failed not just as a genre-label noun but because the _only_ vivid historical source behind it is a hostile outside account — Tacitus describing druids at Anglesey with hands raised, chanting imprecations at Roman soldiers — which is itself the single most recycled druid image in modern pop culture. Digging one layer further, past the "druid" framing entirely and into broader pre-Christian Celtic material, surfaced less-mined imagery: the carnyx, an animal-headed war horn described by contemporary observers as producing an almost unearthly noise, with some evidence of ritual (not just military) use. That search led to "wordless singing, harmony slipping into dissonance and back."

General principle: when reaching for real-world history to ground an entry, check whether the "historical" image is actually the over-familiar one first — accounts filtered through a hostile or outside observer get recycled into cliché just as easily as fantasy tropes do. Widening the search past the first obvious label (e.g. "druids" → "pre-Christian Celtic ritual/sound") can surface material that hasn't already been strip-mined by genre fiction. See Culture_Stem_Design_Process.md's "Inspiration research" section for the same principle applied to literary/game sources rather than history.
