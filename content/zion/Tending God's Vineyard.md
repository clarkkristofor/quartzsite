# Tending God's Vineyard

When we give offerings, we aren't paying dues or covering the church's bills. We're worshiping. When Israel gave generously to build the temple, King David prayed, "All things come from you, and of your own have we given you" (1 Chronicles 29:14). That's the heart of faithful giving: we return to God a portion of what was always God's.

This week's readings are full of vineyards. In Isaiah and Psalm 80, God lovingly plants a vine. In Matthew 21, Jesus tells of a landowner who plants a vineyard, builds a wall, a winepress, and a watchtower, and then leases it to tenants.

The tenants didn't plant a thing. They received a vineyard ready to bear fruit. So do we. Every paycheck, pension, and Social Security deposit comes from God's vineyard. The question isn't "How much of my money does God get?" It's "How do I tend all of it faithfully?"

## A Simple Framework and a Simple Truth

One tool some Christians use is called 10-10-80. Give the first 10 percent. Use the next 10 percent to save or pay down debt. Live on the remaining 80 percent. The exact numbers matter less than the order and the attitude behind them. Giving comes first, as an act of trust. Saving and getting out of debt are both wise investments in hope. The 80 percent we live on isn't the "selfish" part. It's not "mine." It's all God's. It's God's way of providing what we need to live.

When you give money to the church, you have less money. There's no trick to it. But lives are permanently changed for the better: a child who hears about Jesus at Vacation Bible School, a family that gets groceries in a hard month, a neighbor who finds a church home.

That's the harvest of justice and righteousness that Isaiah says God looks for.

## A Healing Way to Live

The tenants in Jesus' parable are consumed by two of the most corrosive emotions: entitlement and resentment. They live out the world's financial advice: "Get all you can. Can what you get. Then sit on your can." Jesus shows where that road leads: theft and violence. Death, not life.

The cures are gratitude and generosity, practices of taking hold of the life Jesus offers: trusting God enough to open our hands, both to give and to receive.

## One Next Step

Whether you give regularly, occasionally, or not yet at all, consider one next step: starting a small, regular gift, growing toward a tithe, building an emergency fund, or prayerfully talking through spending with your household. Shame, blame, comparison, and fear are as devilish as entitlement and resentment. God's abundant love banishes all of them.

As you give today, offer all that you have and all that you are as worship, praying with the psalmist: "Restore us, O God of hosts; let your face shine, that we may be saved." Thanks be to God. —PC
