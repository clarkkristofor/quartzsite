This Sunday marks the beginning of Zion's fall stewardship drive. Next week, you will receive a letter from me with your 2027 estimate of giving card. On Celebration Sunday, November 8, we'll gather all our estimates and offer them to God in worship. These weeks will be your opportunity to prayerfully consider what it will mean for you in 2027 to give to God what is God's.

I have a head start on you, though. Because as I've prepared materials for you for this stewardship campaign, I've been considering what it will mean for me to give to God what is God's in the coming year.

My household gives to God through two congregation, which may be different from yours. Sara, my wife, makes her offerings to St. Paul. I give through Zion. 

I give a proportion of my income. Many years ago I committed to 10% or a tithe. I divided my expected annual income by 10 and set a regular, automatic withdrawal from my bank account. In one of my first years as a pastor, I earned $35,000 before taxes, so I estimated my giving that year to be $3,500. Each week, about $67 went from my account to that of St. Stephen Lutheran Church in South Plainfield, New Jersey. Then, each year after, I considered increasing my giving. Because I received more than my income alone. Like Zion, St. Stephen provided health insurance and pension contributions. In 2026, I'm on track to give $10,000, which is somewhere between 10% of my take home pay and 10% of my total salary and benefits. I haven't increased the amount I gave every year in between, but I wanted to. 

I'm sure you know the feelings. Fear and worry wrestle with gratitude and hope. Giving is always somewhere in between grudging duty and pure delight. On our best days, we feel our cup running over. All we have and all we are is thanks to God! And thanks to God, we get to invest in a heavenly kingdom that the poor inherit, instead of an earthly kingdom that exploits the poor!

"Give to God what is God's," Jesus said. We hear it this Sunday. And this year in particular - as I've written a stewardship letter, and created a stewardship webpage and thought through sermons and newsletter and bulletin for these stewardship weeks - it's been more real than most other years.

I owe my life to God. I lived when others in similar accidents died. I lived. I walked. I avoided surgery. Zion continued paying my salary when I could not work. You were patient when my capacity was low. The health insurance Zion provides is paying my medical bills. You brought my family food and gift cards. You prayed and prayed. 

These months since the accident have included pain and struggle, and also great abundance and love. It echoes in my heart: "Give to God what is God's." In fact, Abdulkader, Zion's former custodian stopped in to the office recently. He had heard about my accident, and he came to see how I was. When he heard how badly hurt I was, and yet how well I am doing now, he told me that Muslims, when they survive calamity, they give zakat - a gift to the poor. "You must give," he told me. And my heart leapt in response. "Of course! Praise God!" So this has to be a year of increase.

Comparison is the thief of joy.
