A year of Mark begins today. And in the Gospel of Mark, Jesus is in a hurry. So is the gospel writer, who had no time for a birth story, but begins with John the Baptist in the wilderness, crying out. Mark shows Jesus's urgency with the word "immediately," which occurs 41 times in the gospel. Though your bible's translators may have spiced up the repetition with "at once," "as soon as" or "just then." Mark's Jesus is a divine first-responder, a combat medic in the middle of a cosmic crisis. Everything is *stat!*

For Mark, to be Jesus's disciple is to *follow*. To keep up as Jesus runs into the danger, and to not be afraid. In fact, for Mark, the opposite of faith is fear. But by that the gospel does not mean the *feeling* of fear. Fear is a natural and necessary human emotion. *Being captive to fear* is something else entirely. 

I count Mark as the gospel that saved me. I was walking home from a bible study on the Gospel of Mark when I felt a jolt of lightning and knew that I didn't have to be afraid of God anymore. And it was my pastor, the leader of that same bible study, who called around noon on September 11, 2001. I had been sitting with my housemate, transfixed by TV images of the collapsing Twin Towers. My pastor asked if my family and I were okay. (We were in New York State, after all.) I was shaken out of my fearful wallow. Fifteen minutes later, I was at the church with my pastor, calling other students on our campus ministry roster whose parents' work addresses were in downtown New York City.

This is what Jesus does in Mark. In a flash, Jesus interrupts fight, flight, freeze and sweeps us into the work of love. *Do not fear, only have faith*, Mark's Jesus says to the synagogue leader whose daughter is dead, pulling him into her ghastly bedroom. And immediately, death becomes life.

And yet, Mark's gospel ends with fear: "...and they said nothing to anyone, for they were afraid." What a gutsy call for the first gospel writer! Certainly it's an empathetic, understanding one. Ending on fear calls us, the hearers, to take courage and have faith, as those women surely must have. Otherwise we would not be hearing about their terrified response to the resurrection.

There's no time to go on about the resurrected Jesus appearing to them and the rest of the disciples. No, we're already late. It's past time to live the good news.

Thanks be to God.

Pastor Clark Olson-Smith
