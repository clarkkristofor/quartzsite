Dearest Zion,

Thank you for your care and support since my accident. You have sent cards and texts, meals and gifts. You have prayed for my healing. You have carried on the ministries of this congregation. I am deeply grateful.

I do not remember the accident. I was turning left into Vander Veer Park on my way to Zion, crossing two lanes of traffic on West Central Park on my electric bike. I never saw the vehicle (a truck, I was told) that struck me. I lost consciousness and do not remember being hit.

I do remember - somewhere between the road and the hospital - becoming aware that I could have been killed. I remember thinking, "This is the kind of experience that makes a person take a hard look at their life and make a change." In a strange way, maybe, it reminds me of the vision of heavenly glory Stephen had in Acts as he was dying. Mine less dramatic but all the same, a gift from God. It prepared me to receive the pain that followed as a reward for surviving.

In the last two weeks, my pain has diminished from excruciating to mostly uncomfortable but occasionally grunt-worthy. Since returning home from the hospital, I've been walking unassisted, including stairs. I drove for the first time just yesterday. I still have a some double-vision, a persistent but improving consequence of the concussion. But I am healing.

When the trauma doctor saw me for the first time in the ER, she said, seeing my injuries on paper, she expected to send me to the ICU. A concussion, nine broken ribs, a broken sternum (the bone not the cartilage), plus a few bonus fractures. But in person, I looked much better than this list would suggest. All other scans ruled out worst-case scenarios. Since then, many others have been amazed at the speed of my recovery.

I have been amazed at the care and support from you and so many - the EMTs, the bystander who called Sara from the scene, the doctors and nurses at the hospital, my colleagues, St. Paul people, Sara, Susannah, and Amos, and the rest of my family and friends.

I am deeply humbled and greatly encouraged. This is medicine as surely as anything that comes from an orange bottle. Soul medicine that lifts us up even when everything else tries to drag us down. What can I give in return except my testimony, which you've heard before, echoing from scripture and in the songs it inspires.

The steadfast love of the Lord never ceases,
his mercies never come to an end;
they are new every morning;
great is your faithfulness.
(Lamentations 3)

I look forward to seeing you very soon and sharing my gratitude and testimony in person.

In Christ, the great healer,

Pastor Clark
