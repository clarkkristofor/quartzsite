Life is not about us. It's about God. Have you heard that saying, "It's not about you"? This is good news that always sounds bad at first. Life is about God. And so is worship. (It's not about us.)

Life and faith are about participation, not perfection. Life and faith are about participation, not *liking it*. Life and faith are about love, and real love has no instruction book, no right answers, no guarantee of return or even fun. Love, much more often than we like, leads to suffering.

"That's how love works," writes Richard Rohr in his book *The Naked Now*, "it leads to the giving up of control, which is my simple definition of suffering: *whenever you are not in control*."

Most parents get this at a visceral level. Both not being in control and how love leads there. You don't have to be parent to get this.

"Love and suffering are a part of most human lives. Without a doubt, *they are the primary spiritual teachers more than any Bible, church, minister, sacrament or theologian*. Wouldn't it make sense for God to make divine truth so available?" says Rohr.

The cross of Christ was God's massive and unconditional surrendering of control. Could there any more dramatic demonstration? Being in control is a human addiction, not God's. Actually, control is an illusion. What we're recovering from is an addiction to the *illusion of control*. God allowed Godself be crucified. For love. "For God so loved the world..."
