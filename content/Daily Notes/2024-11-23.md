I dreamt of falling in love with a crossing guard. She is so captivating. I linger talking with her until the traffic light turns green and the cars want to go again. I hesitate because she's so much younger than I am. I do the math and chart it out: 15 years. My heart aches for her. She's so beautiful. She is so lively in conversation. I think about that: "I need someone who will carry the conversation." Because I am quiet or reserved by nature. I cross her street many times. Every day even. I linger longer and longer. I want to stay with her. Or rather, I want to ask her out. I think she likes me too. It's partly the courage to ask her out and partly to cross the age gap. I'm 36 and she's 21. I imagine bringing her home to my parents. I imagine a (pee-marriage?) session with PB. I imagine saying, "I always looked down my nose at people in relationships like this, but now I get it." I imagine our first date, saying goodbye at the end of it, kissing her, my hands on her waist. That last time I linger so long in the middle of the road with her. My yearning has never been more and I've already decided in myself that I'll ask her out. But I don't. This crossing is on a Maggie intersection, a four lane road that continues in both directions but that in intersected at a T right at her crossing point. I cross from above and too the left of the T, to the left of the 'trunk' of the T. It's a flat and open area. Fields, no trees. But as I walk away from her, it's up a slope, gently, curving to the right a bit. There are usually others around, all of us going about it daily business. 

Even on waking I want to stay near her. I don't want to leave. As if I cross in dream from wakefulness to wakefulness. Is she my feminine self? Who was I at 36 and 21? Susannah was 4 when I was 36 and Amos 1. But I had no children in the dream. I wasn't married. At 21, I was at Cornell. The day before my 22nd birthday I graduated. 15 years. But I'm losing the thread and maybe this line of inquiry is to blame.

Amos arrives, running like a dog.
