Questioning church as it is. In a building. With inaccessible depths. Or how to guide people back into the lost art of scripture. Has to be more than telling them about "how it used to be." Millenia ago. Unmasking the modern mindset.

Then I started wondering, am I questioning my place in it? I think I am feeling...jealousy? About Jennifer Tompos from CAC whose family doesn't participate in a church. "Our children lost so much of us to the church," she said. "We're just not ready" to go back or even consider it. Am I just too focused on the negative? Is there something I'm not bringing to my presence in church spaces? Am I holding back? Like doing pastoral care instead of pastoral agitation?

Yesterday I was blessed by visiting with Mayra. She came to me--already my preferred mode, she was moving in my direction. She had a problem she needed help with--whether to apply for the Gamaliel grant in 2024 or 2025. And I had a wealth of experience here, good questions to ask--it was in my wheelhouse, which was energizing. Plus at the end, I asked about her own discernment/waiting on news about what's next for her. I coached her through leaving QCI, setting boundaries, and moving on. She really appreciated these two things.
1. Is this my anxiety or someone else's? (Did they dump it on me or did I grab it?)
2. To tend to people and not just tasks in the final months. Final one-to-ones, sharing gratitude, allowing the grief. Two lists--what do I need to do? who do I need to visit?
To be that for her, really felt good. It was like, Oh yeah, I'm older and wiser than she is. I have something to share that she values and knows she needs. And I remember being where she is.

How do I invite more of that? I feel like it's a lot of me putting myself out there hoping something lands. Getting little response. Or maybe I want to be a mentor. Have a mentee. I do feel a loss that Mayra is leaving. Great visit but now she's leaving. There's three months anyway. Still there's Hannah.

But what about David? He's grooving on Rohr's books. He comes to me to talk, not to listen. He doesn't have questions, he wants/needs to be heard and seen. Maybe that's what I'm here for. I guess so. And I think I get scared when I don't have something specific to offer. Or feel the--what? superiority? worth? to have something to give. How could I be present to David differently as I let go of that fear? It would be about my presence not about my expertise and wisdom. But could it be anyone? It he wants my presence it's got to be *mine*, you know, my full self. *I serve us so we can serve others.* Right?
