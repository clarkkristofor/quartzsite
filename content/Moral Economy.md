A social psychologist named Gerald Leventhal broke “fair process” into a [checklist](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED142463.pdf), noting that decisions should be consistent, made by neutral parties, based on accurate information, correctable on appeal, and shaped by the voices of the people affected. This is what people want.

Tom Tyler [documented](https://assets.press.princeton.edu/chapters/p8230.pdf) in Why People Obey the Law that people actually accept unfavorable conditions when they believe the process is fair. People comply with systems that they think are neutral, consistent, correctable, and give them a voice. People don’t trust things because they don’t think it’s legitimate or fair.

And that is almost word for word what a Republican pollster [said](https://www.wsj.com/politics/policy/americans-want-populist-policies-that-defy-traditional-political-labels-119b29b9) to WSJ:

Every popular item that we tested in the poll has someone standing between you and a fair price. These aren’t conservative or progressive issues. These are big institutions standing in the way of what voters perceive as fairness.

The WSJ piece documents that people across all party lines want mostly the same policy outcomes, per their poll. It’s not Democrat vs Republican, which is something we should rejoice in.
